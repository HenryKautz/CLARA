Each problem's evidence-free recognition theory — the problem at its recognition
horizon, with the goal literals as the hypotheses, as recognize.sh builds it —
compiled by marginals.sh --solver d4 --project. The projection keeps the drive
actions and the final-slice goal atoms, and FiFO first verifies that they
determine every other atom, so the projected circuit gives exact counts; any
pinned observations are unit literals over the kept actions, so one saved
circuit answers every evidence file by conditioning, without recompiling. CNF
variables and clauses are those of the ground theory d4 is given; projected
variables are the ones it keeps. d4 time is the d4 run alone; total time is the
whole marginals.sh run (reading the theory, the projection check, d4, importing
and saving the circuit, and the marginals of the unconditioned circuit). Circuit
nodes and edges are those of d4's decision-DNNF, before FiFO smooths it. Runs: 1
compilation at a time, each limited to 30 minutes (3×3–7×7) or 20 minutes
(8×8–10×10). SBCL ran with an 8 GB heap (3×3–7×7) or a 12 GB heap (8×8–10×10)
and a 1 GB control stack, d4 with a 64 MB stack (the defaults, 1 GB, 2 MB and 8
MB, are too small for the larger circuits). The 8×8–10×10 runs were timed on the
awake clock (CLOCK_UPTIME_RAW), which stops while the computer sleeps, so a
suspend neither lengthens a time nor times a run out; their records also hold
wall-clock time and CPU time (grid9x9-1, grid9x9-2, grid10x10-4: the computer
slept during the run). The circuits were imported by FiFO's d4 importer as
rewritten to use bit-vector scopes and iterative traversal (FiFO fd0d0b9); the
earlier importer ran out of memory on circuits of about a million nodes.
grid9x9-1, grid9x9-2 were imported by its successor, which builds the circuit in
one pass into packed typed arrays; with fd0d0b9, SBCL's garbage collector
crashed while importing them, after d4 had finished. Model count (verified) is
the number of models of the compiled circuit (all literals set to 1 in the
smoothed circuit), that is, the number of truck walks the theory allows. Every
circuit kept was checked against an independent count of those walks by dynamic
programming over the grid (H-1 steps, each a drive to a neighbouring cell or a
wait, from p1-1 to a goal cell), which agrees with sharpSAT on every problem up
to 7×7 (sharpSAT did not finish an 8×8 problem in 2.5 hours); the two counts
were equal for every circuit in the table. Until d4v2 commit c11210d
(2026-10-16), d4 had a heap overflow (ListLit::setListLit) that from 8×8 on made
it crash, or silently return a wrong circuit, at random. The 8×8–10×10 rows are
from the fixed d4; the 3×3–7×7 circuits were compiled before the fix, which did
not affect them (every one passed the count check). The 3×3–7×7 rows give the
run whose circuit passed the model-count check (grid3x3-1: correct on 2 of 2;
grid3x3-2: correct on 2 of 2; grid3x3-3: correct on 2 of 2; grid3x3-4: correct
on 2 of 2; grid4x4-1: correct on 2 of 2; grid4x4-2: correct on 2 of 2;
grid4x4-3: correct on 2 of 2; grid4x4-4: correct on 2 of 2; grid5x5-1: correct
on 2 of 2; grid5x5-2: correct on 2 of 2; grid5x5-3: correct on 2 of 2;
grid5x5-4: correct on 2 of 2; grid6x6-1: correct on 2 of 2; grid6x6-2: correct
on 2 of 2; grid6x6-3: correct on 2 of 2; grid6x6-4: correct on 2 of 2;
grid7x7-1: correct on 2 of 2; grid7x7-2: correct on 2 of 2; grid7x7-3: correct
on 2 of 2; grid7x7-4: correct on 2 of 2 runs); its d4 time is that run's alone,
not counting failed runs. Their total time is not shown: it was measured with
FiFO's earlier, quadratic evaluator (and partly two compilations at a time); and
no queries were timed on them. Tries counts the runs of d4 on a problem, failed
ones included — a run whose saved circuit a later check found wrong counts as
failed. Query with 2 obs. is the time to recompute every marginal of the saved
circuit conditioned on two observations — the first two drives of a shortest
plan to the problem's alphabetically first destination, pinned to steps 1 and 2
(evidence/PROBLEM-DESTINATION-obs2.txt) — as marginals.sh --circuit --evidence-
file does: the observations are unit literals over kept atoms, so the circuit is
clamped and re-evaluated, not recompiled. It is timed inside SBCL, the median of
5 repetitions, and excludes starting SBCL and loading FiFO (about 1.2 s) and
reading the circuit file (0.001–8.80 s). Each answer was checked: both observed
drives have probability 1, and the final-slice goal marginals sum to 1. FiFO
evaluates the circuit in two linear passes; its backward pass through an AND
node of k children uses prefix and suffix products (an earlier version
recomputed each child's k-1 sibling products, k² multiplications, and took up to
7 s on 7×7 — smoothed AND nodes here have up to 1,007 children). FiFO's circuit-
size cap (*ddnnf-node-limit*, 2,000,000 nodes by default) was raised to
50,000,000 for 8×8–10×10, since a smoothed import of a large d4 circuit exceeds
the default. d4 holds its circuit in memory and writes it only when it finishes,
so an unfinished circuit cannot be counted; its size is given as d4's peak
memory (sampled every 2 s) before it was stopped. No 10×10 problem compiled
within the limit, so larger grids were not tried.
